How a total loss goes today

You did the work. They totaled it. Now you play the reverse card.

Every total loss follows the same script. Here is how it plays out for most shops, and how it plays out for yours with DSM Negotiator.

1

The cat tent writes it short

After the storm, your customer drives through the insurance company's cat team tent and leaves with a low initial estimate they don't understand. Then they bring the car to you and expect you to fix it for that number.

2

It totals on the supplement

You tear it down, photograph the real damage and write an honest supplement. Then the car totals, usually because the insurance company put a low value on it. At a fair value, the repair would sit under the total-loss line. Other times the adjuster simply refuses to save the car, even though you want the work.

3

Then they blame the shop

The adjuster makes the total-loss call and tells you there is nothing you can do, and that they won't pay your storage, teardown or admin fees. Your customer is stuck in the middle with a bill nobody wants to own. That mess is your opening.

4

You play the reverse card

The insurance company used your shop's time, labor and lot to adjust its loss. DSM Negotiator puts your charges in writing, cites your lien rights where your state's law gives them, and sends an itemized invoice for every day and every hour. When they total it, you take it back.

The laws are complex, and the insurance company would rather you didn't know them.

Straight answers.

Is this legal?

Yes. Everything we bill is backed by the law and by attorney-reviewed citations. The software cites claim-handling rules only where they exist for your state, and you review every letter before it goes out. It is not legal advice and not a law firm.

Do I need to be good at negotiating?

No. The software reads what the adjuster sent and drafts the reply. Your job is to read it and press send.

What does it cost?

DSM Negotiator is $1,999 a year, and it includes the full Annual plan of Dent Shop Manager. The training is free.

Does it work in my state?

You can bill for your work in any state. Where we’ve verified the law, letters carry attorney-reviewed citations. Everywhere else they argue from your facts and paperwork and cite no statutes. Lien and storage rules vary by state, so check yours.

What if the customer tries to pick up the car?

The car stays until the total loss bill is paid. The customer signed that in your contract, and that’s what a mechanic’s lien is for. Stay calm, explain the bill goes to the insurance company, and get the adjuster on the phone together.

Why do you give me a step-by-step guide?

Because getting paid starts on day one, not when the car gets totaled. The guide shows you what to get signed, what to document and what to say, so every total loss is one you can bill.

What if the adjuster pushes back?

Paste the reply in. The software reads the new tactic and drafts the next, firmer letter.
